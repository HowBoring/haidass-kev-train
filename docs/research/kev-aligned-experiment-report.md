# Haidass–Kev experimental results

**Scope:** completed local Decision SFT and typed-decision post-training experiments; development-set evidence, not a release/test score. Benchmark reference: the [Kev repository at commit `90990a5`](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/tree/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768), and frozen `jaredpalmer/kev-suites` revision [`a957287`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jaredpalmer/kev-suites/tree/a957287d1c502a4e2e3b9d9d1325c2c6f27f181c). Local results below are linked to their recorded reports; Kev values are *published values*, not our reruns of its weights.

## Executive findings

- **Best local in-distribution (ID) result:** Haidass1.5-143M, LoRA `2e-4`, 4,380-update budget, seed 42, checkpoint selected at update 3,000: **68.35% ID accuracy, 0.396 ID Brier**. Against the original 2,190-update cosine checkpoint (66.46%, 0.425), ID Brier and NLL improve; **out-of-distribution (OOD) accuracy falls from 53.05% to 50.61%**. This is not an across-the-board improvement or a test-set result. [SFT evaluations](../../artifacts/reports/sft-exploration/summary.json) · [original evaluations](../../artifacts/reports/v7-scheduler-comparison/official-fp32.json)
- **Published Kev comparison:** Kev-0.6B reports 80.1% ID, 62.0% OOD, 0.536 OOD Brier on development; Kev-0.8B reports 82.5%, 65.2%, 0.499. The local ID-best checkpoint trails Kev-0.6B by **11.75 percentage points ID** and **11.39 points OOD**; its OOD Brier is **0.025 higher**. The smaller model, training-source subset, and recipe differ, so these are descriptive benchmark gaps, not an architecture-controlled experiment. [Kev models table](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/README.md#models)
- **Typed-decision post-training:** the 300-update RLCD arm attains typed development NLL 1.094, versus 1.104 for continued CE and 1.100 for direct proper loss; all three post-training arms lose Kev OOD accuracy relative to their shared parent. Teacher-agreement accuracy is **not** Kev benchmark accuracy. This small, single-seed engineering comparison does not pass the RLCD entry gate. [Stage-2 results](../../artifacts/comparisons/stage2/summary.json)

## Comparable evaluation contract

The [Kev benchmark](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/kev/benchmark.py) scores **one row per labelled question**. Its headline `clean` block excludes perturbed variants and unknowable questions; it reports micro-averaged accuracy (correct argmax fraction), NLL (negative log probability of the label), and multiclass Brier (mean **sum over options** of squared probability error). Lower NLL/Brier is better. This is **not** a binary-only Brier or a task-macro average. The [implementation](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/kev/metrics.py#L56-L99) supplies the definitions. Kev's `objective` is a **mean of per-task clean NLL**; our SFT checkpoints were selected on that macro measure, not on the displayed micro NLL.

All *local Kev-aligned* table entries use the same pinned official scorer, the frozen `decision-v7` development suite (ID, **1,264 clean labelled questions**, from 1,204 records/1,468 total questions) and `transfer-v4` development suite (OOD, **656 clean labelled questions**, from 764 records/questions), FP32 model forward/master weights, inference temperature **1.0**, and date facts **off**. Accuracy is unaffected by temperature; Brier and NLL are not. The [Kev README](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/README.md#models) says its listed model scores use these development suites, and its Brier values are for **raw logits**, rather than the temperature-calibrated serving default. Comparability therefore uses the same metric definitions and pinned development split; only our checkpoints were rescored by us. No Kev NLL is published in its headline table, so there is no invented cross-project NLL comparison. [Source reconciliation](kev-benchmark-source-notes.md)

**Version note:** the [Kev-0.6B card](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/docs/model-cards/kev-0.6b-qwen3.md) calls its ID development suite `decision-v4`, whereas the README says `decision-v7`; the pinned [v4](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/evals/v4/decision-v4/manifest.json) and [v7](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/evals/v7/decision-v7/manifest.json) manifests list the **same development SHA-256**, despite different training manifests. This does not indicate different development items. The 0.6B card's embedded 0.8B test comparator predates the post-training release; use the pinned README's 0.684, not the older 0.668. [Detailed source note](kev-benchmark-source-notes.md)

The local SFT experiments chose checkpoints on `decision-v7` clean **macro NLL** only; transfer was inspected afterward. The newer SFT and Stage-2 reports did **not** request the locked Kev test split. An [older v6 comparison](../../artifacts/reports/kev-official-comparison/comparison.json) already contains historical test evaluations; therefore **we do not claim the locked test was never read historically**. Those historical test numbers are excluded here and were not used to select these new runs. Selection and repeated inspection of the development suite make the ID scores exploratory, not untouched holdout estimates.

## What we changed, and what the experiments show

1. **v6 → v7 and scheduler.** The old v6-selected checkpoint is historical, not a matched scheduler control. With fixed v7 training data, seed 42, augmentation, and 2,190-update budget, cosine selected at 2,190 (clean development macro NLL **0.7989**) versus OneCycle at 1,800 (**0.8124**); cosine was ahead on both ID and OOD accuracy and OOD Brier in the table. One seed; no general scheduler superiority claim. [Scheduler protocol and result](../../artifacts/reports/v7-scheduler-comparison/summary.json)
2. **SFT learning rate, update mode, and budget.** Four seed-42 4,380-update arms selected on BF16 ID development macro NLL: LoRA `2e-4` **0.7639** (step 3,000); full `5e-5` **0.7835** (step 3,000); LoRA `1e-4` **0.8297** (step 3,000); full `2e-5` **0.8767** (step 4,200). The original 2,190-update LoRA `2e-4` comparator was **0.7989**. The two lower-ranked arms were *not* separately run through the official FP32 scorer; their BF16 numbers must not be placed in the official metric table. [Full first-wave results](../../artifacts/comparisons/sft-exploration/summary.json)
3. **Matched seeds, not just the winning run.** Repeating both long and short LoRA `2e-4` recipes at seeds 42/43/44 gave long-minus-short development macro-NLL deltas **−0.035, +0.027, −0.031** (mean −0.013) and BF16 clean-accuracy deltas **+1.82, +0.79, +0.55 percentage points** (mean +1.05). NLL improved in **2/3**, not 3/3 seeds; the long recipe's best checkpoints occurred at steps 3,000, 2,000, and 1,800 rather than at the 4,380-update limit. At seed 42 its final-step macro NLL **0.8094** was worse than selected-step **0.7639** while training-probe loss continued falling: overfitting evidence, not a converged-win claim. The official-FP32 OOD accuracy of all three long selected checkpoints is **below** the original short seed-42 control. [Matched runs](../../artifacts/comparisons/sft-exploration/summary.json) · [official evaluations](../../artifacts/reports/sft-exploration/summary.json)
4. **Stage-2 typed-decision objectives.** A is the **original 2,190-step v7 cosine checkpoint**, not the ID-best long-SFT checkpoint. B continued CE with replay; C added direct proper loss; D added RLCD with replay. B/C/D ran 300 updates each, used the same parent and independently selected their checkpoints by typed development raw NLL; Kev transfer did not select them. The table separates BF16 typed teacher-target scores (600 development questions) from raw FP32 official Kev retention scores. [Stage-2 recorded design and results](../../artifacts/comparisons/stage2/summary.json)

| Stage-2 arm | Typed teacher-agreement accuracy ↑ | Typed NLL ↓ | Typed Brier ↓ | Kev ID accuracy ↑ | Kev OOD accuracy ↑ | Kev OOD Brier ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: parent, no post-training | 31.17% | 1.493 | 0.372 | **66.46%** | **53.05%** | **0.549** |
| B: CE + replay | 50.00% | 1.104 | 0.185 | 65.66% | 49.70% | 0.553 |
| C: CE + proper loss + replay | **51.17%** | 1.100 | 0.183 | 66.14% | 49.24% | 0.554 |
| D: CE + RLCD + replay | 51.00% | **1.094** | **0.180** | 65.74% | 49.70% | 0.555 |

Stage-2 typed scores target a teacher distribution, so its argmax accuracy measures **agreement with the teacher's top option**, not independently verified correctness or Kev ID/OOD accuracy; its BF16 NLL/Brier are **not** cross-comparable to the official Kev FP32 values. D's typed NLL gain over C is only **0.0069**, in one seed, while D loses 3.35 OOD accuracy points against A. On 180 typed choice-reordering comparisons, calibrated flip rates worsen from A **16.7%** to B/C/D **43.9%/42.8%/45.6%**, so the proper-score gain is not an invariance win. The original parent also has only **1/80** ID relevant policy pairs both correct and **0/64** OOD, which fails the intended RLCD policy-capability entry condition. [Stage-2 metrics](../../artifacts/comparisons/stage2/summary.json) · [parent Kev pair results](../../artifacts/reports/v7-scheduler-comparison/official-fp32.json)

## Interpretation and limits

- **Cross-project comparison is descriptive.** Our pinned 143M Haidass base and 11 selected train sources (ten public sources plus compositional; **no `legacy_policy`**) differ from Kev's Qwen bases and its released `decision-v7` recipe (ten public datasets, **896 generated policy** examples, **1,680 compositional** examples, two-epoch LoRA). We used online augmentation and 2,190/4,380-update budgets; Kev's current Qwen3.5 family also received a short second synthetic training pass. The Kev-0.6B row is the previous Qwen3 generation, not a size-normalized SFT-only ablation. Do **not** attribute the measured gaps solely to architecture, parameter count, or RLCD. [Local train-source contract](../../artifacts/comparisons/sft-exploration/summary.json) · [Kev training and update](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/README.md#training)
- **Primary gap is transfer and compositional policy decisions.** The best local ID score does not carry through to OOD; the original short-run checkpoint remains the conservative local OOD comparator (53.05% accuracy, 0.549 raw Brier). Its 1/80 and 0/64 both-correct policy-pair results prevent a readiness claim. Stage-2 typed gains coexist with OOD regressions and option-order instability. [Official paired-flip definition](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/90990a5fac2995b9faa3190f7d437e84f2067768/kev/contrastive.py) · [local baseline](../../artifacts/reports/v7-scheduler-comparison/official-fp32.json)
- **No broad statistical claim.** Only three matched SFT seeds and one Stage-2 seed; the ID-best first-wave recipe was chosen on the very development set used for its displayed ID score. Paired example intervals are not intervals across training seeds. The official-score table mixes published Kev values and locally rescored predictions, not independently controlled retraining.
